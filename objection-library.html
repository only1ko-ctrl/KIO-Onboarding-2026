Objection Library

Every objection surfaced across the program — with scripted responses, coaching notes, and filters by role, persona, and deal stage.

How to Use This Library

Every objection here was pulled directly from course content across Weeks 1 and 2 — these are not hypothetical. Use this library three ways:

Pre-Call Prep

Filter by persona to anticipate what objections your buyer is most likely to raise before you get on the call.

Post-Call Debrief

Search for an objection you heard on a call. Read the coaching note to understand the root cause and improve your next attempt.

Role Play Practice

Filter by stage and run through every objection at a specific deal phase. Practice responding until each answer lands in under 30 seconds without notes.

A Note on Responses

Scripted responses are starting points — adapt them to your voice. The structure matters more than the exact words. Never read them verbatim on a call.

Cold Call
Cold Call
SDR Cold Call

"Just send me an email."

Response

"Absolutely — I will. Quick question before I let you go: is locating mobile equipment between units something that comes up on your floor?" If yes: you have a qualified conversation. If no: log it and move on.

Coaching Note

This is a brush-off, not a genuine objection — they expect you to hang up. The one-question technique earns 30 more seconds and qualifies pain simultaneously. The question must be use-case specific and pain-surfacing — not "can I set up a meeting." Do NOT simply thank them and hang up.

SDR Cold Call

"We're happy with what we have."

Response

"That's good to hear. Can I ask: when you say 'happy,' are you measuring against where you were before, or against where peer hospitals are benchmarking on response times and asset utilization? Most systems we work with felt the same way — until they saw what a comparable health system was doing with the same data. Is that conversation worth 10 minutes?"

Coaching Note

Signals status quo or incumbent. Don't challenge the happiness directly — reframe the benchmark. They may be happy relative to their own history but behind the market. The peer comparison is the wedge.

SDR Cold Call

"We're happy with our current vendor."

Response

"That's great — who are you with, if you don't mind me asking?" [They tell you.] "How long have you been live?" Under 2 years: log and nurture. 3+ years: "Most systems at that stage are starting to evaluate whether the ROI has held up. Is that a conversation happening at [Hospital Name]?"

Coaching Note

Time-since-deployment is the qualifying variable. Under 2 years = too early, genuine DQ. 3+ years = ROI honeymoon may be fading. Always ask for the implementation date and log it precisely — that date determines your re-engage window.

Response

"Not a priority" is a deflection in most cases. The response works by making the prospect name their actual priorities — then connecting RTLS to those priorities. The rep who can draw the line from "nurse retention" to RTLS in one sentence wins the call extension.

"We just can't afford a capital project of this size right now."

Response

"How many pumps are in your fleet?" [Hear the answer.] "If 20% are over-purchased at your fleet size, that's roughly $X million of capital already deployed against this problem — and you're not getting a return on it. "Can't afford it" assumes a new cost. The reframe: the cost already exists — it's invisible, buried in over-purchasing, rental spend, and productivity loss. Build the ROI from her numbers, not generic estimates. Offering phased deployment concedes the objection — establish the hidden cost first, then phasing becomes a deployment question, not a budget question.

Budget & ROI
CFO Proposal / Close

"How long until we see ROI?"

Response

"30 days from go-live. Go-live is under two weeks. No other RTLS platform can say that." Then layer in the three ROI levers: equipment rental elimination (most hospitals cut 50–76%, often $1M+/year), LOS reduction (0.1 days = $2–3K per patient at scale), and nurse retention ($40–60K per nurse saved).

Coaching Note

The 30-day number is the headline — give it first, then back it up. Never bury it in qualifications. Any one of the three ROI levers justifies the investment at scale; give the CFO all three so they can choose which argument is most defensible internally.
